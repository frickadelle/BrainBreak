# Brainbreak Design

## Physical context

People glance between a shared meeting and a browser on a normally lit desk. A warm, light interface supports a calm break without stealing attention from the puzzle or other players.

## Colour strategy

Restrained: softly tinted paper and ink with a single muted green accent for the brain, primary actions and selected states. Green and purple identify the two word-game teams only; include text labels alongside their colours. Incorrect/correct states use restrained semantic tones and explicit text.

## Typography

Keep the bundled DM Sans for body, controls and data. Retain Outfit sparingly for the brand and main titles. Use smaller fixed heading sizes than the previous marketing layout. Keep instructions readable and paragraphs under 70 characters per line where possible.

## Layout

A compact top bar contains the brain brand, sound toggle and language selector. The homepage places the existing brain illustration beside a simple create/join form. Explain the game in one short line rather than a large marketing section. Game selection uses compact rows with a name, short description and vote count. Gameplay emphasizes the task, shared timer, role, answer controls and progress.

## Components

Use consistent modest radii, light full borders and minimal shadow. Preserve clear selected, disabled, pending, error and focus states. Word boards are the exception to the compact row rule because their spatial cards are the game's interaction. Drawing keeps its large canvas, adds a compact public chat and a correctness table. Right guesses produce a status event without the secret word.

## Motion and sound

Remove decorative page-load and orbit animation. State transitions may use short opacity or colour changes. Respect reduced motion. Generate short local game tones after a user gesture; never start a voice host or require a microphone. Keep the persistent mute control available during play.
